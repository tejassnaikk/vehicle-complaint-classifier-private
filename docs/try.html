Try it yourself

Type a complaint, or load a real one, and watch our simplest model sort it into one of the ten systems. It runs inside your browser.
Which model is this?

This is the MLP (TF-IDF), our simplest model. On the time-based test it has a macro-F1 of 0.8739 and an accuracy of 0.8738. DistilBERT, our best model, scores 0.8945 and 0.8943, but it has too many weights to download into a web page, so it is not here. The scores come from the Results page.

Loading the model: 0.0 of 16.2 MB
Download size: 0.6 MB of vocabulary and 15.6 MB of weights (about 0.1 MB more if you load a real complaint). Nothing you type leaves your device.

How to read the result

  • The bars show the three systems the model finds most likely. The percentages are the model's own confidence and they add up to 100% across all ten systems. A high number is not a promise: the model can be confident and wrong.
  • The highlighted words come from removing one word at a time and checking how the confidence in the top answer changes. Amber words push toward the top answer. Gray words push away from it.
  • Real complaints come from a sample of 200 complaints in our time-based test set, 20 per system. They are the masked text from the published data, and the model never saw them in training. After you predict, the page shows where NHTSA filed the complaint.

Is the browser copy the same as the trained model?

We rewrote the word-count scoring (TF-IDF with single words and two-word phrases) and the three layers in JavaScript. To keep the download small, the weights are stored as 8-bit integers with one scale per row. We then ran the original Python model and this browser code on the same 1,000 test complaints. They picked the same system for 100% of them, and the largest difference in any probability was 0.0028. The report is in results/browser_model_check.json.

Not a safety tool

The model sees only the words you give it. It was trained on one source of complaints, and it knows nothing about your vehicle. Do not use it to decide whether a car is safe. To report a problem, use NHTSA's reporting page.
